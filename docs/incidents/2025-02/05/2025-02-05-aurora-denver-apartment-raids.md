# ICE Raids Target Aurora and Denver Apartment Complexes

## Summary
Federal agents from ICE, FBI, DEA, and other agencies raided multiple apartment complexes across Aurora and Denver in early morning operations targeting alleged members of the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua. The raids displaced families, prevented children from attending school, and sparked controversy over the lack of prior notice to local officials.

## Timeline
- **Before 4:00 AM** - Federal agents begin staging at Best Buy parking lot in southeast Denver and Target on Denver/Aurora border
- **6:00 AM** - Agents arrive at The Edge at Lowry apartments in Aurora, begin knocking on doors
- **6:00-7:00 AM** - Simultaneous operations at Cedar Run Apartments on South Oneida Street in Denver
- **Throughout morning** - Operations continue at Harvard and Quebec complex, family shelter on East Hampden, and complex at Colorado and Mexico near I-25
- **Late morning** - Residents prevented from leaving for work and school; children kept home
- **Five hours later** - Operations conclude at Cedar Run

## Official Accounts

### ICE/DHS Statement
ICE Acting Director Caleb Vitello stated in a video: "We're here today to conduct an at-large enforcement operation looking for Tren de Aragua, the gang members here from Venezuela. Unfortunately, we have to come to the communities because we don't get the cooperation we need from the jails."

### Local Officials
Denver Mayor Mike Johnston stated the city was not given prior notice of the operations and that Denver Police and city authorities were not involved.

Aurora city officials confirmed their police force was not involved: "As we have said numerous times previously, Colorado state law prohibits local governments from engaging in typical immigration-specific enforcement and detention."

## Witness Accounts
Teddy D'Agostino, witness at The Edge at Lowry: "They just started flooding in. They stated that they did have a warrant."

Mark Sewell, Cedar Run resident: "I'm a native Hawaiian, but this right here, this is some BS... we all got each other."

Katie Leonard, activist with Party for Socialism and Liberation: "It's very clear that these [agents] are harassing folks where they live."

Deicy Aldana, resident: "They said that if all their papers are in order, they would be released in three to five hours. They just took them without asking if they had documents."

Ronald Sanchez, resident: "I am not a legal resident, so I didn't answer."

## Context
During his campaign, President Trump dubbed his promised nationwide deportation mission "Operation Aurora," falsely claiming the city had been overrun by Venezuelan gang members. The Edge at Lowry apartments had become a focal point of national attention.

The apartment complex was later closed on February 18, 2025, displacing approximately 40 people (26 adults and 14 children) who were forced to find new housing.
