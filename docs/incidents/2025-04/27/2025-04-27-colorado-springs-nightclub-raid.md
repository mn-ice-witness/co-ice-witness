# Mass Immigration Arrests at Colorado Springs Underground Nightclub

## Summary
Over 300 law enforcement officers from ICE, DEA, and other agencies raided an unlicensed after-hours nightclub called Warike in Colorado Springs, detaining approximately 200 people. More than 100 were placed in ICE custody for immigration violations, while a dozen active-duty military members were among those detained. Despite claims of targeting gang members, no federal or local criminal charges were initially filed.

## Official Accounts

### DEA Statement
DEA Special Agent in Charge Jonathan Pullen stated: "Colorado Springs is waking up to a safer community today." He estimated more than 200 people were inside the nightclub during the operation.

The DEA said the facility had been under investigation for months for alleged drug trafficking, prostitution, and "crimes of violence." Over the past two years, the club had sparked about 170 service calls to police.

### ICE Statement
Officials seized 12 firearms and multiple types of illicit drugs, including cocaine, "pink cocaine" (tusi), and methamphetamine.

### Attorney General Statement
Attorney General Pam Bondi claimed individuals inside were gang "terrorists," though no proof was provided.

### Military Response
U.S. Army spokeswoman: "Each person involved in this incident is presumed innocent until proven guilty." Army Criminal Investigations was on scene.

## Criminal Findings
- **Firearms seized:** 12
- **Drugs seized:** Cocaine, methamphetamine, "pink cocaine" (tusi)
- **Criminal charges filed:** Initially none (as of April 28)
- **Prior criminal convictions:** Only 9 of 72 people arrested that day had any prior criminal convictions according to ICE data
- **Final removal orders:** 18 people were subject to final orders of removal

## Context
April 27, 2025 was the busiest day for immigration enforcement in Colorado since President Trump took office, with at least 72 immigration arrests statewide.

The raid raised concerns about profiling, as advocacy groups noted that most detained individuals had no criminal history and the mass detention appeared disproportionate to actual criminal findings.
