# Initiative 95 Qualifies for November 2026 Ballot: Would Require Police to Notify ICE

## Summary
The Colorado Secretary of State's Office confirmed that Advance Colorado, a conservative political nonprofit, collected sufficient signatures to place Initiative 95 on the November 2026 ballot. The constitutional amendment would require state and local law enforcement to notify the U.S. Department of Homeland Security within 72 hours when they charge someone whose immigration status is in question and the person has a prior felony conviction or is charged with a violent crime. If approved with 55% of the vote, the measure would override existing state laws (HB19-1124 and SB25-276) prohibiting police cooperation with federal immigration authorities.

## Measure Details
- **Initiative 95** would amend the Colorado state constitution
- Requires law enforcement to notify DHS within 72 hours of charging someone whose immigration status is in question, if the person has a prior felony conviction or is charged with a violent crime
- Law enforcement must make a "reasonable effort" to determine immigration status during charging
- Notification requirement applies at the charging stage, not conviction
- Requires 55% voter approval (constitutional amendment threshold)

## Signature Details
- **Total submitted:** 188,861
- **Valid signatures:** 142,299
- **Required:** 124,238

## Backer
- **Advance Colorado**, a conservative political nonprofit led by Michael Fields
- Also backing Initiative 85 (fentanyl penalties)
- No issue committee expenditure disclosures filed

## Current Laws It Would Override
- **HB19-1124 (2019):** Prohibits Colorado police from arresting people based solely on immigration detainers
- **SB25-276 (2025):** Extended cooperation restrictions to local governments and other entities, with $50,000 civil penalties for violations

## Context
The initiative creates a direct conflict with the Democratic-controlled legislature's efforts to further restrict police-ICE cooperation through new bills introduced in the 2026 session (SB26-005 and two additional bills being drafted). Colorado voters will simultaneously decide whether to strengthen or repeal existing restrictions on law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.
