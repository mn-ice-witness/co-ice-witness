# ICE Agents Leave "Death Cards" in Vehicles of Detained; DHS Investigating

## Updates
- **Feb 13** - Congressional deadline for DHS response to OIG investigation demand. No public response from DHS as of this date.
- **Feb 3** - Six Colorado members of Congress [demand independent OIG investigation](https://coloradosun.com/2026/02/03/eagle-county-ice-ace-of-spades-cards/) into Denver Field Office; set Feb 13 deadline for DHS response. ICE says "there is no update" on investigation.
- **Jan 29** - Sen. Hickenlooper calls death cards "cruelty for the sake of cruelty" on Senate floor.

## Summary
ICE agents detained nine Latino men in Eagle County — eight during what advocates called "fake traffic stops" using vehicles with red and blue flashing lights to mimic local law enforcement, and one while walking to a bus stop in Avon. Agents left customized 4x6-inch ace of spades playing cards (bearing "ICE Denver Field Office" text and the Aurora detention center address) inside at least two vehicles of detained individuals. The ace of spades has historical associations with racism and death dating to the Vietnam War. DHS announced an investigation and "unequivocally condemns this type of action and/or officer conduct." Six Colorado members of Congress demanded an independent OIG investigation into the Denver Field Office. Of the nine detained, one was from Mexico and the rest from Central and South American countries including Honduras, Nicaragua, and Peru. All nine were transported to the Aurora ICE Processing Center.

## Official Accounts

### DHS Statement
"DHS unequivocally condemns this type of action and/or officer conduct." Opened investigation into the incident. ICE Office of Professional Responsibility conducting "thorough investigation." As of February 3: "There is no update."

### Congressional Response
Six Colorado members of Congress (Sens. Hickenlooper and Bennet; Reps. DeGette, Neguse, Crow, and Pettersen) sent letter to DHS Secretary Noem demanding: (1) formal briefing on ICE activities in Eagle County, (2) written report, (3) independent OIG investigation into Denver Field Office, (4) written confirmation of disciplinary actions. Deadline: February 13. The lawmakers stated: "Federal agents acting in disguise as local law enforcement is misconduct and should be treated as such."

### Voces Unidas
Alex Sanchez, president and CEO: "We are disgusted by ICE's actions in Eagle County. Leaving a racist death card behind after targeting Latino workers is an act of intimidation." Organization took possession of identical cards found in two separate vehicles by two different families. Voces Unidas is funding legal representation for all nine detained.

## Historical Context
The ace of spades has been used as a symbol of death since the Vietnam War, when American soldiers would leave them on bodies of killed Viet Cong fighters. The symbol has deep associations with racism and intimidation.
