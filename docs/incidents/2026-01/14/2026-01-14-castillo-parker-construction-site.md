# Deonicio Castillo Detained at Parker Construction Site; ICE Was Looking for Someone Else

## Summary
Two ICE agents detained Deonicio Castillo, 53, at his construction worksite in Parker on January 14, 2026, without asking him any personal questions before arresting him. Documents obtained from ICE indicate agents were looking for a different person at the time of the arrest. Castillo, who has lived in the U.S. without status since 1995, has no criminal record, owns a home, and has three U.S. citizen children. He was held 48 days at the Aurora ICE detention facility — including approximately five days after his family paid a $2,500 bond. ICE agents obtained a field warrant for Castillo only after the arrest had already taken place. His case was cited in federal court hearings in March 2026 as an example of problematic ICE detention practices.

## What Happened
ICE agents approached Castillo at his construction worksite in Parker without asking him any questions about his identity, criminal history, employment, family ties, or flight risk before placing him under arrest. Documents from ICE indicated agents were actually looking for a different person at the location. After the arrest, officers obtained a field warrant retroactively. Castillo was transported to the Aurora Contract Detention Facility, where he was held for 48 days.

An immigration judge eventually granted a $2,500 bond. His family paid the bond, but Castillo was not released for approximately five more days. His attorney noted that ICE refuses to process bond payments for an initial 24-hour period after a judge's order, and that families are then experiencing additional multi-day delays before release — a significant change from the pre-2025 norm when bond releases typically took hours.

Castillo's case was entered into federal court proceedings in March 2026 as an example of ICE conducting warrantless arrests without documenting flight risk or danger assessments, in apparent violation of the November 2025 injunction issued by Senior U.S. District Judge R. Brooke Jackson.
