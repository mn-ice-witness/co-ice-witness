# Federal Judge Releases Jose Millan Olivas After Immigration Judge Ignores Habeas Order

## Summary
U.S. Magistrate Judge N. Reid Neureiter ordered the immediate release of Jose Millan Olivas from ICE custody on April 2, 2026, after finding that an immigration judge had failed to follow his prior orders requiring a bond hearing where the government bore the burden of proof. Neureiter wrote that the immigration judge and federal counsel "appeared to be operating under the misapprehension that orders from this Court were advisory or mere recommendations." The case, Olivas v. Baltazar et al., is part of a surge of habeas corpus petitions filed in Colorado federal court by detained immigrants denied proper bond hearings.

## What Happened
Millan Olivas filed a habeas corpus petition in federal court seeking a proper bond hearing. Magistrate Judge Neureiter had previously issued orders requiring a bond hearing in which the government bore the burden of proving the detainee was a flight risk or danger to the community. At the subsequent immigration court hearing, the immigration judge failed to follow those orders — apparently unaware of or disregarding them. Neureiter found the bond hearing was inadequate and ordered Millan Olivas released immediately, finding the government had failed to prove by "clear and convincing evidence" that continued detention was justified.

## Context
Colorado's federal trial court was flooded with habeas corpus petitions in early 2026 after the Trump administration dramatically curtailed the ability of those facing deportation to be released from immigration detention by reinterpreting 1990s immigration law in July 2025. In the first 100 days of 2026, more than 370 detained immigrants in Colorado asked federal judges for bond hearings or release. The Millan Olivas case was one of multiple instances in which immigration courts failed to comply with federal court orders on bond proceedings.
