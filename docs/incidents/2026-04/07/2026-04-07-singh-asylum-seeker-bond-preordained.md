# Federal Judge Orders Release of Asylum Seeker, Flags "Preordained Outcomes" in Colorado Bond Hearings

## Summary
U.S. District Senior Judge William J. Martínez ordered the release of asylum seeker Gurjant Singh from ICE custody, issuing a warning that immigration bond hearings in Colorado have become fundamentally unfair. Martínez wrote that "the mounting evidence that bond determination hearings conducted in immigration court have preordained outcomes has become impossible to ignore" and that immigration judges "often cite little or no reasoning in determining that noncitizens are a flight risk." Singh, who entered the U.S. in November 2023, had been detained by ICE on February 28, 2026, following a traffic stop for a flat tire. He has no criminal record.

## What Happened
Singh was stopped by law enforcement for a flat tire on February 28, 2026, and subsequently detained by ICE. He filed a habeas corpus petition in federal court. In ruling on his petition, Senior Judge Martínez found that immigration bond hearings in Colorado had taken on a pattern of denying bond without adequate justification — often with summary denials lacking reasoning, and with outcomes that appeared predetermined regardless of the individual's circumstances. The court ordered Singh's immediate release.

## Context
Singh's case reflects concerns about bond determinations following the Trump administration's July 2025 reinterpretation of 1990s immigration law that severely restricted detainees' release eligibility. In Colorado, more than 370 detained immigrants filed habeas petitions in federal court in the first 100 days of 2026 — a dramatic increase from 104 in all of 2025 and just a dozen in 2024. Federal courts, which are not under the executive branch's control the way immigration courts are, became the main avenue for challenging detentions. Immigration attorney Hans Meyer said his firm filed 60 habeas cases in six months, compared to six in his prior 19 years of practice.
