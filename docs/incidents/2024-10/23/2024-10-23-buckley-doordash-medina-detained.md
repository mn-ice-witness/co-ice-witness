# Mother with Valid Work Permit Detained While Delivering DoorDash to Military Base; Held 8 Months

## Summary
Carla Azucena Medina Hernandez, a 34-year-old Honduran asylum seeker with a valid work permit, was detained by ICE while delivering a DoorDash food order to Buckley Space Force Base in Aurora. Base security called ICE after Medina, who does not speak English, could not answer their questions. She was held at the GEO Group-run Aurora ICE Processing Center for nearly eight months despite having entered the U.S. legally through the CBP One app. An immigration judge classified her as an "arriving alien," making her ineligible for bond. She won her asylum case in May 2025 and was released on June 9, 2025.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Carla Azucena Medina Hernandez
- **Age:** 34
- **Occupation:** DoorDash driver (former nurse with 12 years experience in Honduras)
- **Citizenship:** Honduran national; asylum seeker with valid work permit (entered U.S. through CBP One app in October 2023)
- **Background:** Fled Honduras in 2023 due to domestic violence and gang threats. Entered U.S. legally through CBP One port-of-entry appointment system with husband Pablo Acosta (29) and two children, Gimena (12) and Mateo (7). Family had pending asylum case since October 26, 2023.

## Timeline
- **Oct 2023** - Medina and family enter the U.S. through CBP One app and begin asylum process
- **Oct 23, 2024** - Medina detained at Buckley Space Force Base entrance while delivering DoorDash order; base security called ICE after she could not communicate in English; transferred to Aurora ICE Processing Center
- **May 8, 2025** - Immigration judge grants asylum; approximately 25 supporters rally outside Aurora GEO detention facility; government reserves right to appeal on one issue (one-year filing deadline)
- **Jun 9, 2025** - Released from Aurora detention facility after nearly eight months; government appeal period expires

## Official Accounts

### ICE/Buckley Space Force Base
Buckley Space Force Security Forces notified U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement in Denver, who arrested Medina on scene. ICE classified her as an "arriving alien," a designation that made her ineligible for bond release.

### Immigration Court
An immigration judge found extraordinary circumstances justified her late asylum filing and granted asylum in May 2025.

## Witness Accounts
Medina described her detention: "It's degrading. I've never been in a prison. I've never had this experience." She was confined with approximately 24 others in one room with no outdoor access.

Her husband Pablo Acosta did not visit in person during the eight months: "If they detain me, the kids stay alone — so I don't go."

On her release, Acosta said: "To tell the truth, I feel very nervous, happy. I can't believe it, still, that this has happened."

Andrea Loya, executive director of Casa de Paz: "It is a community win to have Carla free."

Jennifer Piper, AFSC program director: "The dedication of her husband, Pablo, and the support of community made a huge difference in her case."
