# Denver Police Chief Says Officers Will Intervene Against ICE Excessive Force

## Summary
Denver Police Chief Ron Thomas said officers will step in if they witness federal immigration agents using excessive force during operations in the city. Thomas also stated that DPD will not share immigrant information with federal authorities and wants immigrants to feel comfortable reporting crimes. The statements come as former ICE agents told the Denver Gazette they expect Denver to become a focus of enforcement after operations in Minneapolis conclude.

## Sources
1. Denver Gazette (Feb 5, 2026): [Denver police chief: Officers will intervene when they see 'excessive force' by ICE](https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/02/05/denver-police-chief-officers-will-intervene-when-they-see-excessive-force-by-ice/)
2. Colorado Politics (Feb 5, 2026): [Denver officers would step in over ICE excessive force, chief says](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/02/05/denver-police-chief-officers-will-intervene-when-they-see-excessive-force-by-ice/)

## Official Accounts

### Denver Police Chief Ron Thomas
"If we see a federal officer who we believe is using excessive force, then I think our expectation is to intervene in that situation."

Thomas also stated that DPD will not share information about immigrants with federal authorities: "We want them to know that we certainly support them. We want to protect their rights as well."

Thomas noted that Denver's peaceful anti-ICE protests demonstrate the city does not need federal intervention: "We have demonstrated the fact that, not only can we manage these very peacefully — even very large demonstrations."

### Former ICE Agents (unnamed)
Former ICE agents told the Denver Gazette they expect the Trump administration to focus on Denver after Minneapolis operations conclude. One said: "Once Minneapolis comes to a conclusion, I expect Denver to be in their cross hairs."

## Context
Thomas's statements come amid escalating ICE operations in Chicago, New York, and Minneapolis, and weeks of large anti-ICE protests in Denver. Aurora, home to an ICE processing center and detention facility, has said it is willing to collaborate with federal partners. Colorado's "sanctuary" laws prohibit state officials from sharing personal identifying information with immigration authorities.
