# Governor Polis Takes Hands-Off Approach to ICE Pushback as Other Democratic Governors Lead

## Summary
As Democratic governors in other states actively lead efforts to push back against ICE enforcement, Colorado Governor Jared Polis is taking a notably hands-off approach, declining to appear at a Capitol rally where advocates and lawmakers championed ICE restrictions. This contrasts sharply with governors like Massachusetts' Maura Healey, who personally unveiled proposed protections. Polis said he hopes to see "guardrails" put on DHS at the federal level and emphasized Colorado's existing laws, but signaled he may not support the package of new immigration enforcement bills being advanced by state legislators. In his January 2026 State of the State address, Polis called Trump's enforcement "costly and often cruel" and noted only 25% of detained immigrants had criminal convictions, a shift from January 2025 when he supported federal immigration enforcement. Democratic lawmakers suggested Polis could allow bills to become law without signing them.

## Key Details

### Polis's Position
- Hopes to see "guardrails" on DHS at the federal level
- Emphasizes Colorado's existing immigration protections rather than pursuing new ones
- Said he is "always willing to work with legislators" but is "mindful of what we already have on the books"
- Absent from Capitol rally where advocates and lawmakers championed ICE restrictions

### Evolution of Polis's Stance
- **January 2025:** Supported federal immigration enforcement
- **January 2026 State of the State:** Called Trump's enforcement "costly and often cruel," noted only 25% of detained immigrants had criminal convictions
- **February 2026:** Taking cautious approach, not actively leading pushback

### Contrast with Other Governors
- Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey personally unveiled proposed protections
- Other Democratic governors are actively leading anti-ICE efforts
- Polis notably absent from advocacy events

## Official Statements

### Governor Polis
"While I am always willing to work with legislators...I am mindful of what we already have on the books."

On recent deaths in Minneapolis: "We are committed to going after criminals, no matter where they are from, but that's not what we're seeing with these actions."

Said he has been "disturbed" by enforcement in Minneapolis and has demanded "thorough investigations" into the killings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti.

### Lawmakers
Rep. Lorena Garcia suggested Polis could allow bills to become law without signing them if he opposes them directly.

Democratic lawmakers note Polis's hesitation contrasts sharply with peer governors adopting similar protections statewide.

## Context
The governor's stance comes as Colorado Democratic legislators advance a package of bills to regulate ICE operations, including allowing lawsuits against federal agents, banning masks during enforcement, requiring identification, and increasing oversight of detention facilities. The legislative package is proceeding without clear gubernatorial support.
