# Colorado Attorney General Launches Online Tool to Report ICE Misconduct

## Summary
Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser's office launched a new online complaint filing system allowing residents to report alleged misconduct by federal agents, including ICE personnel. The system, which officially launched at the end of January, has received over 180 complaints in the past six months but has resulted in zero prosecutions. Once submitted, complaints are reviewed and referred to relevant state or federal oversight bodies when appropriate. Reported misconduct includes violent arrests of families, shattered car windows with children inside, and people pulled from vehicles and thrown to the ground. Advocates have raised concerns about the system's effectiveness, noting that no data will be published or shared publicly and questioning whether it amounts to a "campaign stunt" given Weiser's gubernatorial campaign.

## Key Details

### How the System Works
- Residents file complaints online through the Attorney General's office
- Complaints are reviewed and, when appropriate, referred to relevant state or federal oversight bodies
- Unlawful activity that can be reported includes: use of excessive force, unlawful searches or arrests, wrongful detentions, interference with voting, and other civil rights violations

### Complaints Received
- Over 180 complaints regarding alleged federal agent misconduct in the past six months
- No prosecutions have been initiated from these complaints
- Colorado Immigrant Rights Coalition (CIRC) has independently documented many cases through its hotline

### Types of Misconduct Reported
- Violent arrests of families
- Shattered car windows with babies inside
- Community members pulled from vehicles and thrown to the ground
- Use of excessive force (including the Durango incident where an agent threw a protester to the ground)
- Warrantless detention and arrests

## Official Statements

### Attorney General Phil Weiser
"One of the big issues...is the gathering of evidence. The Attorney General of Minnesota went to court to preserve evidence that local law enforcement needed access to."

Weiser acknowledged that the state does not have direct authority over federal agents but said documenting patterns of alleged misconduct is a critical first step toward accountability.

### Advocates
CIRC Co-Director Gladis Ibarra: "This is what they were elected to do. We have been seeing things ramp up."

Ibarra also expressed skepticism: "My personal take is that this is a campaign stunt," referencing Weiser's gubernatorial campaign.

RMIAN Director Mekela Goehring: "There really has been a change in the number of folks who have been arrested and detained, it's quadrupled in the last year."

## Concerns About Effectiveness
- No complaint data will be published or shared publicly
- Zero prosecutions from 180+ complaints raises questions about outcomes
- State lacks direct authority over federal agents
- Advocates worry the system may give false hope to discouraged community members
- System represents only one piece of a larger enforcement picture
