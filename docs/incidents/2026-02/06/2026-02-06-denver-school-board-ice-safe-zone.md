# Denver School Board Considers Policy Designating Schools as ICE "Safe Zones"

## Updates
- **Mar 8** - KUNC reports ["Children are afraid"](https://www.kunc.org/news/2026-03-08/children-are-afraid-advocates-say-denvers-policies-to-shield-students-from-ice-must-be-stronger) as advocates say existing Denver school policies must be stronger; community members testify about fear and confusion following immigration enforcement.
- **Mar 5** - DPS holds public hearing. Latino advocates push for a [broader policy](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/03/05/advocates-push-denver-school-board-to-pass-ice-policy-even-if-duplicative/) that would protect not just school buildings but also bus stops and school-sponsored events, even if it duplicates existing measures. The board's next formal reading is set for March 19.

## Summary
The Denver Public Schools board is considering a policy to designate all district and charter school properties as "Safe Zones and Sensitive Locations" that restrict immigration enforcement activity. The proposal would bar ICE agents from school property without a federal judicial warrant, prohibit staff from cooperating with immigration authorities, and prevent school police from arresting students if deportation would result. The policy was drafted at the request of Latino community organizations and board President Xóchitl Gaytán. At a March 5, 2026 public hearing, advocates pushed for an even broader policy covering bus stops and school-sponsored events.

## Policy Details
The proposed policy would:
- Designate all DPS and charter school properties as "Safe Zones and Sensitive Locations"
- Deny ICE agents access to property, records, or video without a federal judicial warrant
- Prohibit questioning or detention of students by federal agents without court orders
- Refuse to comply with ICE detainer requests
- Protect student immigration status information from disclosure
- Prevent school resource officers from sharing student data with federal agents
- Bar school police from ticketing or arresting students if deportation would result
- Assist staff with DACA protections if the program is rescinded

## Official Accounts

### Board President Xóchitl Gaytán
Championed the effort, citing community concerns about heightened immigration enforcement activity.

### Latino Community Organizations
Movimiento Poder and the Latino Education Coalition helped develop the policy and requested the board consider it, describing "a sense of urgency" as immigration enforcement has increased.

## Context
Denver Public Schools previously filed and then dropped a lawsuit seeking to block Trump administration ICE guidance regarding enforcement near schools. Colorado is also among states where ICE agents have drawn weapons near schools and smashed car windows with children inside vehicles.
