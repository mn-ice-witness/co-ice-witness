# Denver Flock Surveillance Cameras Used Nearly 1,400 Times for Immigration-Related Searches

## Summary
Police nationwide accessed data from Denver's Flock license plate reader cameras nearly 1,400 times for ICE-related or immigration-related searches in 2024 and 2025, despite Colorado state law prohibiting law enforcement from sharing personal information with federal immigration authorities. Denver pulled its data from Flock's national directory on April 8 after city council opposition, but a federal agent subsequently used Loveland police's Flock account to conduct 26 immigration-related searches of Denver data between April 3-25. Flock Safety has also admitted that federal immigration agents have direct access to tracking data through a pilot program with U.S. Customs and Border Protection, contradicting previous company statements.

## Key Details
- **Flock cameras** are automatic license plate readers that capture vehicle "fingerprints" including model, plate number, and visible details like scratches and bumper stickers
- **1,400 immigration-related searches** of Denver Flock data conducted by law enforcement nationwide in 2024-2025
- **26 immigration-related searches** of Denver data conducted by a federal agent using Loveland police's Flock account (April 3-25)
- **Denver data removed** from Flock's national directory on April 8 after city council opposition
- **Flock admitted** to operating a pilot program giving Border Patrol direct access to tracking data, contradicting previous claims
- Denver still shares Flock data with other Colorado departments
- Cameras installed at 70 intersections citywide

## Current Safeguards (since October 2025)
- Denver contract extended through March 31, 2026
- Immigration and reproductive healthcare searches prohibited
- $100,000 fine for sharing data with federal government for immigration enforcement
- Referral to Colorado Attorney General for prosecution if data is shared with immigration authorities
- Federal agents on Denver task forces cannot search the license plate database

## Related Legislation
- **SB26-070 (PEEPS Act):** Bipartisan bill by Sen. Judy Amabile (D-Boulder) would restrict government access to historical location data, shorten retention from 30 days to 4 days, and make unlawfully obtained data inadmissible
