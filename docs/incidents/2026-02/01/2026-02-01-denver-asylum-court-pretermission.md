# Trump Administration Uses Pretermission to Deny Asylum Cases en Masse in Denver Court

## Summary
The Trump administration is increasingly using a legal strategy called "pretermission" — denying asylum applications without a merits hearing — to remove asylum seekers from Colorado by sending them to third countries such as Honduras and Ecuador. Homeland Security filed more than 500 pretermission motions in Colorado in 2025, including 322 in Denver Immigration Court and 203 involving detainees at the Aurora ICE detention center. Immigration attorneys report that nearly all of their asylum clients in Colorado have been told their cases should be terminated and they must seek refuge in countries they may have never visited.

## Sources
1. Denver Post (Feb 1, 2026): [In Denver courtroom, Trump administration pushes to send asylum seekers to third countries](https://www.denverpost.com/2026/02/01/colorado-asylum-immigration-court-pretermission/)

## Key Details
- **What is pretermission:** An asylum application is denied without a merits hearing, typically citing asylum cooperative agreements with foreign nations
- **Scale in Colorado:** 322 motions in Denver Immigration Court and 203 involving Aurora detainees in 2025
- **National scale:** More than 6,700 pretermission approvals nationwide in 2025; surged from 38 in March to over 1,000 per day by mid-December
- **Target countries:** Honduras and Ecuador (both carry U.S. State Department travel warnings)
- **Cooperative agreement countries:** Belize, Ecuador, Guatemala, Honduras, Paraguay, and Uganda

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Number:** Hundreds of asylum seekers in Colorado affected
- **Citizenship:** Asylum seekers from various countries
- **Background:** Asylum seekers in both Denver Immigration Court and Aurora ICE detention center; many have no connection to the countries they would be sent to
- **Status:** Cases being dismissed without merits hearings; some remain in ICE detention awaiting an unknown fate

## Official Accounts

### Jose Rosales, Denver Immigration Attorney
"It seems like a deliberate attempt for the government to strip them of their right to file asylum."

### Monique Sherman, Rocky Mountain Immigrant Advocacy Network
"They're just blindsided by this."

### Judge Brea Burgie, Denver Immigration Court
Every asylum case called up in her courtroom was met with a pretermission motion filed by Homeland Security attorneys.

## Context
Homeland Security is not required to prove that the country it proposes to send an asylum seeker to is willing or able to accept them. Immigration attorneys report many clients are left in ICE detention awaiting unknown outcomes. Colorado has 78,000 pending immigration cases and the worst rate of attorney representation in the nation.
