# Colorado Forced to Share Medicaid Data with Federal Government, Including ICE

## Summary
Denver Health distributed notices informing patients that due to federal changes within Medicaid, personal information including citizenship status, address, phone number, date of birth, and Medicaid ID may be shared with ICE through the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). The data sharing affects people enrolled in Emergency Medicaid or pregnant individuals enrolled through Cover All Coloradans who are not lawfully residing in the U.S. Colorado and nearly two dozen states sued last summer to challenge the disclosure, but a federal court denied the motion and is allowing data sharing in limited circumstances while litigation continues.

## Data Being Shared
CMS may share the following with ICE for individuals not lawfully residing in the U.S.:
- Citizenship and immigration status
- Address
- Phone number
- Date of birth
- Medicaid ID

## Affected Populations
- People enrolled in **Emergency Medicaid** (covers acute, life-threatening conditions such as severe injuries, heart attacks, labor complications; includes hospital, ambulance, and dialysis services)
- Pregnant individuals enrolled in Medicaid through **Cover All Coloradans**
- Does not apply to U.S. citizens or lawful residents enrolled in Medicaid

## Legal Challenge
- Colorado and nearly two dozen states filed suit last summer to challenge the Medicaid data disclosure
- A federal court denied the states' motion to block data sharing at the end of 2025
- Data sharing is proceeding in limited circumstances while litigation continues
- The CMS-ICE Information Exchange Agreement was established in July 2025 and made public in January 2026 through lawsuit discovery

## Hospital Responses
- **Denver Health:** Distributed patient notices; declined interview but stated it "remains a place that is safe for patients to continue receiving care"
- **UCHealth:** "We do not ask for immigration status" and has made no changes to patient information protections
- **Children's Hospital Colorado:** Continues safeguarding information per HIPAA
- **Colorado Hospital Association** (Cara Welch): Hospitals will continue serving all patients regardless of immigration status

## Official Accounts

### DHS Position
DHS Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin said CMS and DHS are "exploring an initiative to ensure that illegal aliens are not receiving Medicaid benefits that are meant for law-abiding Americans."

### Colorado Department of Health Care Policy and Financing
A spokesperson confirmed federal law requires the department to share certain basic information with CMS because Medicaid is jointly financed and administered by the state and federal government, but stated: "We cannot directly control what CMS shares."

### Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser
Criticized the initiative, emphasizing that sensitive data should not reach immigration agencies.

### Advocates
Gladis Ibarra of the Colorado Immigrant Rights Coalition: "It's devastating to think that people need to choose between their health and their safety."

Bethany Pray of the Colorado Center on Law and Policy: "People should not have to choose between giving birth in a hospital and wondering if that means they risk deportation."

### State Legislators
Rep. Elizabeth Velasco (D-Glenwood Springs), who sponsored a bill prohibiting state and public entities from sharing data with ICE unless a criminal investigation is involved: "I believe this is eroding trust in government."

## Context
The data-sharing policy represents a reversal of prior CMS assurances that Medicaid information would not be shared with immigration enforcement. Advocates worry it will deter both undocumented immigrants and other non-citizens from seeking emergency medical care, including life-threatening conditions and prenatal care.
