# Denver City Council Committee Advances Mask Ban for Law Enforcement Including ICE

## Summary
A Denver City Council committee unanimously advanced a proposed ordinance that would ban all law enforcement officers — including federal ICE agents — from wearing face coverings while detaining or arresting people. The ordinance, sponsored by Councilwomen Shontel Lewis and Flor Alvidrez, would also require officers to display visible identification including names and badge numbers. Violations could result in citations carrying fines up to $99 or up to 300 days in jail, with Denver Police authorized to arrest non-compliant officers. The full council is expected to take an initial vote within two weeks, with a final vote possible as soon as March 2. The city attorney raised concerns about whether the identification visibility requirement — which would require ID readable from 25 feet — is constitutional, and a similar California law targeting only federal officers was struck down by a federal court; Denver's proposal applies equally to all law enforcement to avoid that issue.

## Key Details

### Ordinance Requirements
- Prohibits law enforcement from wearing masks or face coverings while detaining, arresting, or restraining people, and while in city facilities
- Requires visible identification including name and badge number
- Applies equally to local, state, and federal law enforcement

### Exemptions
- Undercover operations
- SWAT/tactical operations
- Emergency responses
- Religious garb
- Helmets and transparent face guards
- Medical masks
- Protective eye gear

### Penalties
- Citations carrying fines up to $99
- Up to 300 days in jail
- Denver Police authorized to arrest violators

### Timeline
- Committee approval: February 11, 2026 (unanimous)
- Full council initial vote: expected within two weeks
- Final vote eligible: as soon as March 2, 2026

### Legal Concerns
City Attorney Miko Brown raised constitutional and operational questions about the identification visibility requirements, suggesting sponsors may need to make changes.

City Clerk Paul López cited election integrity concerns, referencing suggestions of federal agents at polling centers.

## Context
The ordinance comes in response to ICE agents wearing masks during immigration enforcement operations across Colorado, a practice that has generated widespread criticism. A similar California law that applied only to federal officers was struck down by a federal court as discriminatory; Denver's ordinance applies equally to all law enforcement to avoid that legal vulnerability.
