# Garfield County Sheriff Accused of Illegally Coordinating with ICE to Deport Immigrant

## Summary
The nonprofit law firm Towards Justice sent a cease-and-desist letter to Garfield County Sheriff Lou Vallario on February 25, 2026, accusing his office of illegally coordinating with ICE to arrest and deport an undocumented immigrant outside a Glenwood Springs Walmart in June 2025. The man had no federal or state criminal charges and was later deported from an Aurora detention center. Advocacy group Voces Unidas and Towards Justice allege a pattern of Colorado law violations by the sheriff's office, including unlawful ICE access to the county jail and illegal sharing of personal information with federal immigration authorities.

## Key Allegations

### Walmart Arrest (June 2025)
A Garfield County deputy and an ICE agent arrested an undocumented immigrant outside the Glenwood Springs Walmart. The deputy then drove the man approximately 40 miles to the town of De Beque, where he was turned over to ICE custody. The man was held at the Aurora ICE detention center and subsequently deported. He had no federal or state criminal charges at the time of arrest.

### Alleged Pattern of Violations
Voces Unidas and Towards Justice allege the Garfield County Sheriff's Office has:
- Allowed ICE agents into the county jail to detain immigrants ahead of their scheduled release
- Held individuals past their release dates by manipulating bond time
- Facilitated direct transfers of inmates to ICE without a judicial warrant in nonpublic jail areas
- Shared "personal identifying information" with ICE — including names, dates of birth, addresses, and immigration status — outside of criminal investigations, in violation of Colorado law
- Participated in the SPEAR task force in ways that allegedly violated state immigration law

## Official Accounts

### Towards Justice / Voces Unidas
Alex Sánchez of Voces Unidas and Toree Lindblad of Towards Justice said the man arrested at Walmart had no criminal charges and was deported through the illegal coordination. The organizations are demanding the sheriff's office halt the practices and comply with Colorado immigration law.

### Sheriff Lou Vallario
Through spokeswoman Shannon Stowe, Vallario said the office works "with ALL law enforcement agencies...to the degree I legally can." He previously claimed to comply with state law regarding release timeframes. In a direct email, Vallario wrote: "I will not comply with this, if this draft is the end result. You can call me 'defendant' in a lawsuit to correct this pro-criminal stupidity."

### Garfield County Attorney's Office
Said it would review the cease-and-desist letter before the county takes any action.

## Legal Context
Colorado's Colorado RISE Act (SB21-131) broadly prohibits local law enforcement from participating in civil immigration enforcement activities and blocks sharing personal identifying information with ICE outside of criminal investigations. The Garfield County Sheriff's Office participates in the SPEAR (Sheriff's Public Engagement and Response) task force, which Towards Justice alleges has been used to channel immigration enforcement in violation of state law.
