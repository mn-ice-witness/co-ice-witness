# Denver Mayor Johnston Issues Executive Order Restricting ICE Actions, Authorizing Detention of Agents Using Excessive Force

## Summary
Denver Mayor Mike Johnston signed Executive Order 152 on February 26, 2026, directing Denver police to intervene when ICE agents use force that "could cause death or serious bodily injury," to document federal agents with body cameras, and to investigate criminal allegations against ICE officers. The order bans ICE from staging or operating on city property without judicial warrants, requires police to protect peaceful protesters, and directs that investigations of ICE misconduct be referred to the Denver district attorney or Colorado attorney general for potential prosecution. Johnston said: "We seek not to provoke but to protect."

## Key Provisions of Executive Order 152

- **Intervention duty:** Denver police must provide life-saving aid and intervene when ICE force "could cause death or serious bodily injury"
- **Body cameras:** Officers must document ICE agents using body cameras when "tactically safe"
- **Investigation:** DPD will investigate criminal allegations against ICE agents; cases referred to DA or AG
- **City property ban:** ICE prohibited from staging on or using city property without judicial warrants or valid court orders
- **Protest protection:** Police directed to protect peaceful protesters during federal operations and de-escalate confrontations
- **Prosecution pathway:** ICE misconduct referrals go to Denver DA or Colorado AG for potential prosecution

## Official Accounts

### Mayor Mike Johnston
"We seek not to provoke but to protect." Johnston framed the order as protective for all Denver residents: "Denver residents deserve people by their side...those you can trust."

Johnston noted the order responds to chaotic enforcement operations elsewhere, including in Minnesota where two people died at federal agents' hands and U.S. citizens were detained.

## Context
The executive order represents an escalation from Johnston compared to Governor Jared Polis's more measured approach, and complements state legislative efforts to regulate ICE. Johnston previously signed a 2023 ordinance requiring body cameras on Denver police during operations and a 2024 ordinance restricting ICE access to Denver facilities. Executive Order 152 goes further by explicitly authorizing police to detain ICE agents who use excessive force.
