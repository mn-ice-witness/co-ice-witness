# Trump Budget Bill Threatens Immigrant Health Care Access in Colorado

## Summary
The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (H.R. 1), signed into law in July 2025, will fundamentally reshape health care access for immigrants in Colorado beginning October 2026. An estimated 7,000 lawfully present immigrants in Colorado — including refugees and asylees — will lose Medicare and Medicaid eligibility. Denver Health estimates 20,000 of its Medicaid patients could become uninsured. The bill also imposes mandatory work requirements that could affect 375,000 Coloradans and cuts federal reimbursement for emergency Medicaid, shifting costs to states and hospitals. Health care providers warn patients will delay care until emergencies, driving up uncompensated care costs at safety-net clinics statewide.

## Key Provisions of H.R. 1 Affecting Colorado
- **October 2026:** Refugees and asylees lose Medicare and Medicaid eligibility
- **Mandatory work requirements** for some Medicaid beneficiaries (~375,000 Coloradans potentially affected)
- **Emergency Medicaid cuts:** Federal reimbursement (currently 50-90%) reduced, shifting costs to states and hospitals
- **Elimination of enhanced premium subsidies** affecting marketplace coverage

## Colorado-Specific Impacts
- **7,000 lawfully present immigrants** expected to lose Medicare/Medicaid coverage
- **20,000 Denver Health Medicaid patients** could become uninsured
- **$118 million** currently spent on emergency Medicaid for noncitizens (out of $14 billion total state budget)
- **OmniSalud program:** Eligibility reduced from 12,000 people (2025) to 6,700 (2026); 44% of 2025 enrollees lost renewal eligibility
- **20 community health centers** operating 247 clinic sites statewide face budget pressure

## Colorado Programs Affected
- **Cover All Coloradans:** Medicaid expansion for immigrant children under 18 and pregnant individuals
- **OmniSalud SilverEnhanced Savings Plan:** State-subsidized private insurance for undocumented immigrants; already reduced enrollment

## Health Provider Responses
- **Denver Health:** Estimates 20,000 patients could become uninsured; spokesperson Deydra Bringas confirmed preparations
- **Doctors Care (Littleton):** Health policy director Laura-Elena Porras warned patients will "delay their care...until it's an emergency"
- **Colorado Consumer Health Initiative:** Spokesperson Priya Telang expressed concerns about cascading impacts

## Context
The bill represents the latest federal policy affecting immigrant health care access in Colorado, alongside the separate Medicaid data-sharing with ICE issue (see Feb 11, 2026 incident). Advocates fear the combined effect of coverage loss and data-sharing will deter immigrants from seeking emergency medical care, including life-threatening conditions and prenatal care.
