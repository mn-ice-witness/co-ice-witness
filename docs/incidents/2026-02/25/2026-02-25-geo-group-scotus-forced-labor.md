# Supreme Court Rules Forced Labor Lawsuit Against Aurora ICE Contractor Can Proceed to Trial

## Summary
The U.S. Supreme Court ruled 7-2 on February 25, 2026, that a class-action lawsuit alleging GEO Group forced immigration detainees at the Aurora ICE Processing Center to perform sanitation work under threat of solitary confinement can proceed to trial. Writing for the majority in *GEO Group, Inc. v. Menocal* (No. 24-758), Justice Elena Kagan held that GEO Group's federal contractor defense "provides a potential defense to liability, not an immunity from suit," meaning the company cannot use it to block the case from going forward. The underlying lawsuit alleges detainees were forced to clean common areas without pay, with sanctions up to 72 hours in solitary confinement for refusal, and were paid only $1/day for other labor such as food preparation and laundry.

## Key Findings

### What the Court Ruled
The Court held that the *Yearsley* doctrine — which provides federal contractors a defense when sued for actions taken at the government's direction — is "a defense to liability, not an immunity from suit." Because it is not an immunity, GEO Group cannot immediately appeal the denial of that defense mid-litigation. The ruling did not decide whether GEO violated forced labor laws; it only allowed the case to proceed to trial.

### The Allegations
Detainees at the Aurora ICE Processing Center brought two claims:
- **Forced sanitation work:** The "Sanitation Policy" required all detainees to clean common areas without pay. Refusal could result in escalating sanctions, up to 72 hours in solitary confinement.
- **Voluntary Work Program:** Detainees who performed other labor (food preparation, laundry, facility maintenance) were paid $1/day.

The lawsuit alleges these practices violate the federal Trafficking Victims Protection Act and Colorado's unjust enrichment law.

## Context
The Aurora ICE Processing Center is Colorado's only officially acknowledged ICE detention facility, operated by GEO Group under federal contract. The original class-action lawsuit was filed in 2014. The Tenth Circuit previously sided with detainees, and GEO Group sought Supreme Court review. The case will now proceed toward trial in lower courts on the merits of the forced labor claims. Rep. Jason Crow (D-Aurora), whose district includes the facility, issued a statement welcoming the ruling.
