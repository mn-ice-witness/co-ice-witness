# Afghan Asylum Seeker Who Helped U.S. Fight Taliban Detained by ICE, Judge Orders Release

## Summary
Mohammad Ali Dadfar, a 37-year-old Afghan asylum seeker living in Louisville with his wife and four children, was detained by ICE at an Indiana weigh station while working as a truck driver. Dadfar had previously worked for the National Directorate of Security under the U.S.-backed Afghan government and fled after the Taliban retook power in 2021. He entered the U.S. legally in June 2024 through the CBP One app with valid humanitarian parole (until June 2026), obtained work authorization, and was employed as a truck driver while his asylum case was pending in Denver. He was held at the Greene County Jail in Missouri for nearly two months. On December 1, a federal judge ruled ICE violated its own parole regulations and the Fifth Amendment's due process clause, ordering his immediate release and return to Colorado.

## Official Accounts

### Federal Court Ruling
U.S. District Court in Missouri's Western District found that ICE violated its own regulation governing the termination of parole. Under federal rule, parole can be automatically revoked only in certain cases, such as if a person leaves the U.S. or if the parole period expires. Dadfar's parole had not expired, and he had not departed the country. The judge ruled ICE violated the Fifth Amendment's due process clause, finding that Dadfar had a "core liberty interest" in remaining free. The government provided no evidence he was a flight risk or posed a danger.

### Political Response
Rep. Joe Neguse called the detainment "a disgrace, and yet another example of this administration's draconian immigration policies."
