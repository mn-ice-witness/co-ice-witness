# Advocacy Report Documents Malnourishment, Forced Labor, and Medical Neglect at Aurora ICE Detention Facility

## Summary
A coalition of advocacy groups released a report on March 2, 2026, documenting conditions at the GEO Group-operated Aurora Contract Detention Facility based on testimony from 31 detainees interviewed between September 2025 and February 2026. The report describes insufficient and nutritionally inadequate meals forcing detainees to spend $80-$100 per week on commissary items, a "volunteer work" program paying $1 per day with mandatory participation enforced through punishment, denial of medical treatment for serious conditions, and new psychiatric diagnoses that advocates say could harm detainees' immigration cases. GEO Group disputed the allegations.

## Key Findings

### Food and Nutrition
Detainees describe meals as overly processed, extremely low-calorie, and lacking nutrition. One detainee described a lunch as "a third cup of beans, quarter cup canned corn, a few pieces of lettuce, a half piece of bread, and a baby's spoon sized serving of something unidentifiable without color." Another reported going eight months without fresh fruit or vegetables. Guards allegedly refused to deliver food to sick individuals.

Detainees require $80-$100 per week to supplement facility food with commissary items — and a 30-minute phone call costs $5, equivalent to five days' wages.

### Labor
The "volunteer work" program pays $1 per day, with mandatory participation enforced through punishment. Advocates compared the system to "sharecropping."

### Medical Care
Medical concerns were the most commonly documented issues in the report:
- 38 mentions of new diagnoses during detention (including schizophrenia diagnoses that detainees say they had never experienced before and that could harm their immigration cases)
- 17 mentions of inadequate treatment for existing conditions
- 13 mentions of prescription medication denial
- Detainees reported receiving only ibuprofen for serious conditions including chest pain and vomiting
- 82 mentions of living condition issues including extreme temperatures

### Living Conditions
Detainees report extreme temperatures, overcrowding, and abusive guard behavior.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Number:** 31 detainees interviewed
- **Citizenship:** Various (mix of undocumented immigrants and asylum seekers)
- **Status:** Detained at Aurora facility

## Official Accounts

### GEO Group (Facility Operator)
"GEO strongly disagrees with these allegations, which we believe are instigated by politically motivated outside groups." The company states it provides qualified medical professionals, ICE oversight, around-the-clock medical care, legal and family visitation, dietician-approved meals, and religious services.

## Context
The Aurora Contract Detention Facility holds over 1,000 immigrants and is operated by GEO Group, the same company contracted to operate the proposed new Hudson (Big Horn) facility. GEO's operating contract at the Aurora facility expires in October 2026. The American Friends Service Committee, one of the report authors, is also a plaintiff in the ACLU lawsuit (Ramirez Ovando v. Noem) challenging ICE's warrantless arrest practices in Colorado.
