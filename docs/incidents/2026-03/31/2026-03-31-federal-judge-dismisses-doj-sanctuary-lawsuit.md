# Federal Judge Dismisses DOJ Lawsuit Challenging Colorado and Denver Sanctuary Policies

## Summary
U.S. District Judge Gordon P. Gallagher dismissed the Justice Department's lawsuit against Colorado and Denver on March 31, 2026, ruling that compelling state and local officials to assist in federal immigration enforcement violates the 10th Amendment. The lawsuit had targeted several state laws limiting cooperation with ICE and a Denver ordinance barring ICE from entering jails without a judicial warrant. The ruling stated: "The Federal Government does not have the power to direct the Colorado legislature or Colorado state and local officials in such a fashion." The decision aligns Colorado with similar federal rulings in Illinois and New York rejecting comparable DOJ claims.

## Laws Challenged

The DOJ lawsuit targeted:
- Colorado state laws limiting local law enforcement's ability to enforce federal immigration statutes
- State restrictions on sharing personal information with ICE
- A Denver ordinance preventing ICE from entering jails without a judicial warrant

## Ruling

Judge Gallagher ruled that the federal government cannot commandeer state and local officials to implement federal immigration policy under the 10th Amendment's anti-commandeering doctrine. The judge noted that while states cannot be compelled to participate in federal immigration enforcement programs, voluntary cooperation remains permissible.

## Context
The ruling aligns Colorado with recent federal decisions in Illinois and New York rejecting similar DOJ claims. Colorado's "sanctuary" policies had been a target of the Trump administration's broader effort to pressure states into assisting with immigration enforcement. The decision protects the state laws passed during the February 2026 legislative session and existing Denver ordinances.
