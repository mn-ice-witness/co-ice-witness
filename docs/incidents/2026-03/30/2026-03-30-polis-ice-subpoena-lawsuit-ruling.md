# Judge Refuses to Dismiss Lawsuit Against Gov. Polis Over ICE Subpoena Compliance

## Summary
Denver District Judge A. Bruce Jones refused to dismiss a lawsuit against Gov. Jared Polis on March 30, 2026, ruling that Polis's motion to dismiss was "untethered to any rule of procedure" with an "absence of any legal authority." The lawsuit, filed in June 2025 by former labor division director Scott Moss, alleges Polis directed him to comply with an ICE subpoena seeking home addresses, email addresses, and phone numbers for 35 workers — despite Colorado law prohibiting state agencies from sharing personal data with federal immigration authorities unless related to a criminal investigation. Polis later reversed course and sought to block the data release, but then moved to dismiss the case. Mediation is scheduled for mid-April 2026; depositions of the governor are planned beforehand.

## Background

ICE issued a subpoena to Colorado's Division of Labor Standards and Statistics requesting personal information — including home addresses, email addresses, and phone numbers — for 35 workers. Polis directed then-Director Scott Moss to comply with the subpoena, claiming it related to a federal human trafficking investigation.

Colorado state law (SB21-131) prohibits state agencies from sharing personal identifying information with federal immigration authorities unless the request is related to a criminal investigation. Moss refused to comply and filed a lawsuit against the governor in June 2025, alleging Polis violated state law by directing the compliance.

Polis subsequently reversed course in early 2026 and sought to permanently block the information release — but also moved to dismiss Moss's case. The judge rejected the dismissal motion.
