# Soliman Family Detained Despite FBI Clearing Them

## Updates
- **Jan 21** - Immigration judge [denies bond](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/family-detained-ice-colorado-bond-denial/), reversing September ruling. DHS called children "terrorist adjacent."
- **Sep 2025** - Immigration judge initially grants bond, finding family "not a danger to the community."

## Summary
ICE detained the wife and five children of Boulder attack suspect Mohamed Soliman, holding them for over seven months in a Texas detention facility despite the FBI clearing them of any knowledge of the attack. An immigration judge denied their bond in January 2026, with DHS calling the children "terrorist adjacent."

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Hayam El Gamal (wife), Habiba Soliman (18), and four younger children
- **Ages:** 5 to 18 years old
- **Citizenship:** Egyptian nationals, asylum-seekers with expired visas
- **Background:** Family was living in Colorado Springs, children attending local schools. Applied for asylum after visas expired.

## Timeline
- **Jun 2025** - Boulder attack occurs; ICE detains family shortly after
- **Sep 2025** - Immigration Judge Justin Adams rules family eligible for bond, finding them "not a danger to the community, and not a flight risk"
- **Nov 2025** - Board of Immigration Appeals remands the bond decision
- **Jan 19, 2026** - Press conference with 100+ community supporters advocating for family's release
- **Jan 21, 2026** - Same judge reverses decision, denies bond

## Official Accounts

### DHS/ICE Statement
DHS attorneys argued in court that the eldest daughter Habiba and her teenage brother should not be eligible for bond because they were "terrorist adjacent."

### FBI Position
An FBI agent testified that the family knew nothing of the attack plans.

## Witness Accounts
Family attorney Eric Lee stated: "Now this same immigration judge has reversed his prior decision and determined that the family is likely to abscond" from justice.

Supporters stated: "Not only is there no evidence to support that the mother and children had any knowledge of their father's actions...they have proved the opposite."

Habiba Soliman, 18, described detention conditions at the South Texas Family Residential Center as "inhumane."
