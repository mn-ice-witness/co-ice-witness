# Family Detained at Immigration Court After Judge Dismisses Their Case

## Summary
A family of three — a mother, father, and small child — were detained by plainclothes ICE agents inside the Byron Rogers Federal Building moments after an immigration judge dismissed their removal case. The agents did not identify themselves, pulling out badges and saying "You are coming with us." Attorney Emily Brock, who witnessed the arrest, was refused four minutes to brief the family on their rights. The family was transferred to a facility in Centennial for processing and then to an out-of-state family detention center. Advocates called it the first known arrest of a family inside a courthouse in Colorado and a policy shift that could discourage immigrants from attending lawful court proceedings.

## Witness Accounts
Emily Brock, deputy managing attorney at the Rocky Mountain Immigrant Advocacy Network, witnessed the arrest: "They didn't identify themselves, they just pulled out their badges and said, 'You are coming with us.'" She described the child clinging to his father's neck, "terrified."

Jennifer Piper of the American Friends Service Committee stated she had "never seen this in more than 20 years of doing this" and had "never seen one single person detained at immigration court."

## Official Accounts

### ICE
Steve Kotecki, a Denver ICE spokesman, did not return a request for comment. ICE stated it was detaining people subject to fast-track deportation authority.

### Legal Context
On Inauguration Day 2025, Trump rescinded the policy that had directed ICE to avoid enforcement at courthouses. Colorado law prohibits ICE from making civil arrests in or around state courthouses, but federal courts are not governed by that state law.
